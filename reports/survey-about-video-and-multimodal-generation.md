# Comprehensive Survey on Video and Multimodal Generation: Architectures, Open Ecosystems, and Evaluation Paradigms

## TL;DR (3-5 bullets, each ending with citation [n])
- Video generation has transitioned from cascaded 3D U-Nets to scalable Diffusion Transformers (DiT) and autoregressive tokenizers like Sora and VideoPoet [1][2].
- Unified multimodal frameworks such as Chameleon and NExT-GPT bridge discrete/continuous modalities for any-to-any generation [3][4].
- Open-source video models like LTX-Video and CogVideoX achieve realtime and high-resolution synthesis through 3D causal VAEs and expert transformers [5][6].
- Comprehensive benchmarks like VBench, EvalCrafter, and VideoScore align automated evaluation with human perception while addressing safety and trustworthiness [7][8][9].

## Background (definitions, significance, cite foundational work [n])
Video and multimodal generation represents a cornerstone in artificial intelligence, bridging computer vision, natural language processing, and audio synthesis to create coherent sensory streams from textual and multi-modal prompts. Early generative models relied heavily on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and variational autoencoders limited to low-resolution, short-duration clips. The advent of diffusion models and autoregressive transformers revolutionized the field, enabling high-fidelity text-to-video (T2V) and image-to-video (I2V) synthesis [1]. Foundational architectures like VDM and Imagen Video demonstrated the efficacy of diffusion processes in pixel and latent spaces, while subsequent scaling laws established that joint spatial-temporal modeling underpins realistic motion generation [1]. Simultaneously, multimodal generative systems aim to unify perception and generation across text, image, video, and audio modalities, establishing arbitrary cross-modal translation capabilities [3][4].

## Foundational Architectures and Diffusion/Autoregressive Models for Text-to-Video (T2V)
The architectural evolution of text-to-video generation is characterized by a paradigm shift from traditional 3D U-Nets and cascaded pixel/latent frameworks to scalable Diffusion Transformer (DiT) foundations [1]. Models like Open-Sora introduce Spatial-Temporal Diffusion Transformers (STDiT) that decouple spatial and temporal attention to efficiently model complex video distributions, leveraging Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) and QK-normalization for stable half-precision training [10]. Similarly, large-scale architectures like Sora and W.A.L.T utilize window-restricted self-attention, alternating spatial/temporal blocks, and latent space diffusion to scale to high resolutions and multi-second durations [1]. In parallel, autoregressive decoder-only Transformer approaches such as VideoPoet unify multimodal inputs—including text, images, video, and audio—through a sequence-to-sequence tokenization approach, enabling zero-shot high-fidelity motion generation via a two-stage pretraining and task-specific adaptation protocol [2].

## Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and Unified Audio-Visual-Text Generation
Unified multimodal generation frameworks bridge disparate data modalities through either early-fusion discrete tokenization or modular cross-modal adaptors. Chameleon pioneered early-fusion mixed-modal foundation models trained on ~10T tokens of interleaved text, image, and code data, quantizing images into discrete tokens to allow a single uniform Transformer to handle arbitrary sequences without modality-specific encoders [3]. Extending this philosophy to any-to-any generation, AnyGPT translates diverse continuous modalities into discrete tokens using neural codecs, unifying them within an autoregressive LLM backbone via next-token prediction [11]. Alternatively, modular frameworks like NExT-GPT connect an LLM core with multimodal adaptors and open-source diffusion/audio decoders, employing modality-switching instruction tuning to achieve efficient any-to-any generation while freezing core LLM parameters [4]. More recent advancements such as LatentOmni and SkyReels-V4 leverage unified latent reasoning spaces and dual-stream Multimodal Diffusion Transformers (MMDiT) to preserve dense temporal grounding for synchronized audio-visual generation, inpainting, and editing [12][13].

## Open-Source Ecosystems, Efficiency Optimization, and Acceleration Techniques
To democratize high-resolution video production, the open-source community has developed highly efficient inference and training paradigms. LTX-Video bridges Video-VAE and denoising transformers by shifting patchification to the encoder, achieving a spatiotemporal compression ratio of 1:192 paired with denoising-decoder fusion to recover high-frequency details [5]. Operating at faster-than-realtime speeds, LTX-Video produces 5-second 24 fps videos in ~2 seconds on an NVIDIA H100 GPU and supports GGUF quantization for low-VRAM deployment [5]. Similarly, CogVideoX introduces a 3D causal VAE and an expert transformer with adaptive LayerNorm and 3D full attention, stabilizing convergence and establishing a commercial-grade open-source baseline [6]. To mitigate the quadratic complexity of 3D attention, training-free methods like Sparse VideoGen dynamically classify attention heads to accelerate inference [14], while quantization frameworks like QVGen and Quant VideoGen tackle low-bit quantization and 2-bit KV-cache compression for long-form autoregressive video generation [15][16].

## Evaluation Benchmarks, Safety, and Trustworthiness
Evaluating video and multimodal generation has evolved beyond superficial distribution distances (such as FID and FVD) that exhibit low correlation with human perception. VBench established a hierarchical evaluation suite dissecting video quality into 16 distinct dimensions grouped into video quality and video-condition consistency, incorporating human preference annotations [7]. Concurrently, EvalCrafter introduced 700 diverse prompts and 17 objective metrics to expose critical limitations in camera motion control and text rendering [8]. To automate fine-grained human feedback, VideoScore utilizes models initialized from multimodal architectures (built on top of the VideoFeedback dataset) via generative and regression scoring to act as a robust automated metric that outperforms traditional feature-based baselines [9]. Furthermore, VBench++ expands evaluation into image-to-video tasks and integrates rigorous trustworthiness protocols, employing specialized safety classifiers such as NudeNet, Stable Diffusion Safety Checker, and Q16 to detect implicit NSFW content, cultural bias, and safety violations [17].

## Trends and Open Problems
Despite remarkable strides in video and multimodal generation, several critical challenges remain unsolved. First, maintaining long-range physical consistency and temporal coherence over extended durations (exceeding 30 seconds) continues to challenge current diffusion and autoregressive architectures [1][5]. Second, balancing the trade-off between extreme spatiotemporal VAE compression and fine-grained high-frequency detail retention requires advanced denoising-decoder designs [5]. Third, ethical risks surrounding deepfakes, copyright infringement, and demographic bias necessitate robust, automated safety alignment and watermarking protocols integrated directly into model training pipelines [17]. Future research directions point toward unified omnimodal latent reasoning frameworks, training-free acceleration sparsity, and reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF) driven by automated MLLM reward models [12][14][9].
